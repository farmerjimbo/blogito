a prince of cups

February 9, 2026 · Gusanito

by Gusanito
//

Your Prince of Cups cried of laughter, when you met him, at the old house—the one our friends used to pass down to each other for generations before the landlord sold its memories & the lights that glowed red at night & the cigarette butts fossilized in the crack between the oven and the wall.

¿Could you help me paint the picture?

We were at the round table, near midnight, sharing a joint, Strawberry-Blonde to your right, Auburn to my left. Tell that story again, the one about your high school trip to central America they begged you. Ok ok, yes ¿the one when my teacher had an affair with one of the locals, and she didn’t come back with us? you grinned. ¿What? Your Prince of Cups pinched the bridge of his nose trying to hide his teary eyes, and this made you smile. When you drove him to his St. Louis apartment, he asked you where you’re from and you told him We’re from the same place. He returned the smile: We’ll definitely see each other again, then, and when he saw your car drive away, Your Prince of Cups didn’t see it again until you drove him to the park by Lake Minnetonka. Your Prince of Cups was hurt, in crutches, yet he was the one who helped you hop onto the hammock, by his side, where you read Japanese and Irish love stories aloud. I’m from a family of witches, you told him, and you asked him to pick a card from your tarot deck: the King of Cups. I’m backpacking around Italy for the next two months ¿will you stay here? you asked. Not sure if I’d be able to walk very far, and this made you smile, especially when you were at the base of the Alps & you wrote your prince’s name on the snowy peaks & you borrowed black ink from the nearby cows’ spots to write his name on your journal & you traced his name with your pinky on every beer’s foam & you found him in your deck of cards, every time you played a king & you carved his name onto the metal ring you smithed for him. You gifted Your Prince of Cups that ring the day you drove him back from the Missouri river clad in pink rocks—you were silent mermaids by the waterfall. Your Prince of Cups played with the ring on his index finger; he held your hand under the round table, your friends none the wiser, like in your favorite love stories—the ones with secrets. When you moved to Florence to work at the metal shop, leaving Your Prince of Cups, you didn’t want him to stop loving you, and you told him this in Siena, when he flew to that cobblestone plaza where you shared a spliff—You’re the one for me, you confessed, and he held your hand because I can’t say it back, not confidently, not when we’ve known each other for a few months, not at my age, and not when you live so far. You didn’t smile when you told him Never text me back even though we both wanted him to, you wanted Your Prince of Cups to fight for you, but he listened to you, and you didn’t smile when his plane took off; you didn’t smile on the beaches or behind your welding helmet. You regretted what you asked the Prince of Cups to do, and you finally called him, and you told him the truth—that you still loved him. ¿Why are you telling me this years later? I love someone else, don’t call me again—but you did, again and again: I think about you most days ¿Can we coexist? —and Your Prince of Cups didn’t have the strength to tell you No, because you’ve been singing the same song so many years later, so he gave you a Maybe. Maybe.

Your Prince of Cups can’t remember what you look like. He looks at your picture, over and over, but your face remains a vapor. What he doesn’t want to tell you is that years after he last saw you, he went to Lake Superior, he uttered your name, and he let its water wash it away. \\
