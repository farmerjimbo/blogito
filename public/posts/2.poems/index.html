in the scalloped white morning I look for you

February 9, 2026 · Dylan Bourne

by Dylan Bourne
//

In the scalloped white morning I look for you, you came
and left before I woke. In my dream: everyone’s face intricate
with grief. Not yours. Your face scraped clean by the
temperature of this rock, by the white stuff floating in the air.

Now my mom half-rigid above the kitchen counter in
green middle day, filling out your death paperwork,
unsure what to be stressed about now without your moans,
your multiplying organs stopped, your shrinking face stopped,
yours fevers cooled, and your worries fully complete and realized:
everything unfinished, only a few videos made, and you left us,
your children, alone with the big stones in the yard, unsure which

is in its right place, which is too heavy to move. But at least
you stopped dreaming of the good-tasting food you could
no longer eat. Mom’s putting you away now. The few videos you
completed were for us, for future key moments in our life. What
did you know about how the days would feel?—summer is still warm,
I see children everywhere, busy parents. But not the same world. In the vacant
street I’ve lined up all your words, your expressions too and the order of your sentences
perfect down the row, but the force that made it all is gone, and no new words come. \\
