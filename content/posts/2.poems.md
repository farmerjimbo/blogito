by Dylan Bourne  
//  
<br>  

<p class="poem">
In the scalloped white morning I look for you, you came<br />
and left before I woke. In my dream: everyone’s face intricate<br />
with grief. Not yours. Your face scraped clean by the<br />
temperature of this rock, by the white stuff floating in the air.<br /> 
<br>  
Now my mom half-rigid above the kitchen counter in<br />
green middle day, filling out your death paperwork,<br />
unsure what to be stressed about now without your moans,<br />
your multiplying organs stopped, your shrinking face stopped,<br />
yours fevers cooled, and your worries fully complete and realized:<br />
everything unfinished, only a few videos made, and you left us,<br />
your children, alone with the big stones in the yard, unsure which<br />
<br>
is in its right place, which is too heavy to move. But at least<br /> 
you stopped dreaming of the good-tasting food you could<br />
no longer eat. Mom’s putting you away now. The few videos you<br />
completed were for us, for future key moments in our life. What<br />
did you know about how the days would feel?—summer is still warm,<br />
I see children everywhere, busy parents. But not the same world. In the vacant<br />
street I’ve lined up all your words, your expressions too and the order of your sentences<br />
perfect down the row, but the force that made it all is gone, and no new words come. \\ <br />
</p>
